GTM Intelligence Platform

A lightweight customer data platform built on dbt. It takes product usage, Salesforce and a third-party company feed, three systems with no key in common, and resolves them into one account-level view a sales rep can work from. This page is the write-up: what was asked, what the data actually looked like, how I modelled it, and what it produced.

01

The problem

A GTM team needs to know which accounts to call and why. The evidence for that lives in three places that share no identifier, and two of them disagree with the third about what a company even is.

Source 1

Product usage

Who does what inside the product, and through which surface: web UI, SDK, CLI, MCP. Users, orgs, projects, objects, page views, API keys, trace volume.

Source 2

CRM

Salesforce accounts and a partial map from product org to account. Domains and LinkedIn URLs are present on some accounts and missing on others.

Source 3

External intelligence

A vendor feed of company news, product launches and technology detections. It carries no Salesforce id at all, only a messy website, a LinkedIn URL and a LinkedIn-reported domain.

What the brief asked for

PartAskWhat that means in practice
AResolve users to orgs to Salesforce accounts deterministically, with an ambiguity flag. Classify product activity by surface, honestly. Build the internal spine, and be explicit about which surfaces the data can and cannot prove. Some orgs will not map, and that has to be a state rather than a bug.
BNormalise identifiers into comparable join keys and resolve the external feed onto accounts. Keep the unresolved. A full URL and a bare domain have to meet. LinkedIn URLs vary by scheme, www, country sub-domain, trailing slash and query. Net-new prospects are an output, not an error.
CA unified view reps can act on, built to extend. One place to read an account, and a shape that takes a fourth source without a rewrite.
DTests you would actually run, plus a memo. Tests that assert the reasoning, not just the row shapes.
The constraint that shaped everything. The brief states it plainly: favour an unresolved mapping over a bad merge, carry a source and a confidence on every resolved mapping, and set a flag on conflicts rather than guessing. I took that literally. Nothing here merges on a similarity score, and where evidence disagrees the record goes to a review queue with the reason attached instead of a winner being picked.

Eight questions, and what the data said

#QuestionAnswer, with the evidenceWhat it changed
1Is one user_id one person? No 9 logins, 8 distinct emails. grace@acme.com appears as both u_grace and u_grace_dup. Merge on exact lower-cased email. Keep both ids and point them at one person_id.
2Does a person belong to one org? No Two logins sit in two orgs, and u_frank's two orgs roll up to two different accounts (Globex and Umbrella). A person resolves only when all of their orgs agree. Frank gets no account and is flagged.
3Does every org have a CRM account? No Five of six map, and one of those five only through a rule named org_c_fallback. Confidence comes from how the link was made. An unmapped org is a prospect.
4Do objects belong to their creator's org? Yes All 31 objects sit in a project belonging to an org their creator is a member of. project → org → account is safe, and safer than the user, who may sit in several orgs.
5Does anything name the client or surface? No Nothing records a client, surface, user-agent or key id. source_url, mapping_source and key_hash are near-misses; none is it. Surface has to be inferred from side-effects, and labelled with how strong the inference is.
6How much origin evidence exists? 17 of 22 experiments carry repo_info. Across all 31 objects, 12 carry neither JSON field. Those 12 are unknown. They are never rounded up to "UI".
7Do the external identifiers join as delivered? No Not one vendor website equals a CRM domain literally. Ten non-null websites arrive in seven different shapes. Normalise both sides in one model, then match. That is also how hooli.com turns out to belong to two accounts.
8What window does the data cover? Everything ends 2026-07-27. Traces run 8 days and rise by exactly 0.7 GB a day for every org, so they are clearly synthetic. Every window measures from a frozen as_of_date, so a run in six months gives the same answer.
The finding that cost the most modelling time. Question 5. Without a client or a key id on any object, "which surface" stops being a lookup and becomes an inference with a confidence attached. That one gap is why there is a rules seed, why every activity row carries an attribution class, and why two of the four surfaces can never be reported as usage at all. Section 7 works it through.
04

Approach and architecture

Three layers, each allowed to do exactly one kind of work. Raw lands and types the data. Cleansed makes things comparable, relates them, decides, then classifies, in four passes that never mix. Unified shapes what was already decided and adds no logic of its own.

One normaliser

int_match_keys builds the Salesforce keys and the vendor keys in the same CTEs. Two models cannot normalise the same thing differently, because no other model normalises anything.

One extension point

A new source, whether intent data, support tickets or billing, is one more union branch in fct_account_signals. The 360, any alerting and any reverse-ETL pick it up without being touched.

One definition per number

Trace growth was being recomputed in three marts. It now lives in int_account_trace_trend and is read three times, so the three cannot drift apart.

Why the materialisations differ

Staging is views. A rename and a cast over one seed. Copying it would buy no speed and create a second copy to drift.

Cleansed is tables. Resolution is the expensive, opinionated part. Computing it once means every mart reads the same answer instead of each recomputing the match ladder and possibly disagreeing after a change.

Unified is views only. Always current, nothing to schedule, and no question about when a mart was last refreshed.

Rules live in seeds, not in SQL

Three CSVs hold the platform's judgement calls so they can be read and changed by someone who does not write SQL: ref_surface_signals (what each signal proves and how confidently), ref_org_mapping_sources (contract 0.95, billing parent 0.85, fallback 0.50) and ref_external_match_rules (LinkedIn 0.90, domain 0.85, LinkedIn-domain 0.75, with company name marked can_resolve = false).

The day a real CLI usage signal exists, adding one row to the first of those makes CLI measurable without a code change.

Model-by-model analysis

Twenty models carry the logic. For each one: what it decides, and what it actually produced on this data. Row counts are read back from the built warehouse, so the result column is measured rather than expected.

Cleansed — where the decisions are made

ModelGrainRows What it servesResult on this data
Why four passes and not one big model. Each cleansed pass is allowed one kind of work: make rows unique and comparable, relate them, decide, then classify. A model that both resolves and scores is one nobody can debug six months later, because when a match looks wrong you cannot tell whether the key was built badly, the candidate was generated wrongly, or the precedence rule fired. Splitting them means each failure has exactly one place to look. int_signal_company_match_candidates deliberately produces every possible link and refuses to choose. Choosing is the next model's only job.

Identity resolution

Part A

Login to person to org to account. Deterministic at every hop, and the moment two pieces of evidence disagree the result is no account plus a reason, never a best guess.

Org to account

Confidence is inherited from how the link was made, not assigned by feel. Every org survives, mapped or not.

OrgPlanAccountViaConfStatus

Person to account

A person's confidence is the weakest org link behind them. Two accounts means none.

PersonOrgsAccountConfStatus

Which key, and why

Login to person: lower(trim(email)). The inbox is the credential, so an exact match is a fact rather than an inference. Not the name, and not the email domain: acme.com alone covers three different people here.

The multi-org problem

Frank belongs to Globex and Umbrella. His objects still attribute correctly, because they carry a project and a project has exactly one org. Only his browser events, which carry no org, stay unattributed. That is the honest answer, not a rounding error.

What a user_id on an object really is

The credential that made the call, not necessarily a human. Bob's 12 experiments run nightly between 02:01 and 02:12 with ci_run_id = gha-10xx, an author of ci-runner and a key named ci-service. That is a GitHub Action, not a person.

Members, with the human and machine split kept visible

Read Bob's row across. sdk_evidence = no_evidence sits next to sdk_evidence_any_actor = usage_proven. His key genuinely drove SDK traffic, so the account really is using the SDK, but he is not a person using the product and a rep chasing active users should not see him as one. The machine stays visible without being mistaken for a user, and a test fails the build if that ever inverts.
07

Surface attribution

Part A

Nothing in the data names a surface, so every claim here is an inference. The job was to make each inference explicit: which surface, whether it proves usage or only interest, how it was attributed, and how much to trust it.

Where the 31 objects actually landed

Nineteen objects can be tied to code. Twelve carry no origin evidence whatsoever and are recorded unknown. Rounding those twelve up to "UI" would have roughly doubled the apparent UI footprint on the basis of nothing.

Every activity row, by signal

The nine signals below are rows in ref_surface_signals, not branches in a CASE statement. Each records what it proves and, the column that matters more, what it cannot.

What can be proven

UI is deterministic: a logged-in page view is a page view. SDK is deterministic for 18 of 31 objects and heuristic for one more, resting on repo_info and CI tags, with org-level corroboration from trace ingestion.

What cannot

CLI usage is not observable. The wizard is a browser event and a key name is free text. MCP is the same: a docs click is not a connection. Both are capped at intent_only, and cli_and_mcp_never_claimed_as_usage fails the build if anyone changes that.

Account by surface adoption

The denominator is known product members, meaning people with a membership in an org mapped to the account. It is not company headcount, and the column names say so.

AccountSurfaceMeasurableMembers ProvenIntentStatusProof sits at% proven
The row that needs the caveat. Initech reads sdk: usage_proven at 0% of members. Both are true: the org demonstrably ships traces programmatically, and no individual member can be shown to have written a line of code. evidence_level says org rather than member for exactly that row, and a test forbids org-level proof from ever being reported as member usage. Without that column the pair looks like a bug. With it, it is the finding.
08

External to account resolution

Part B

The core of the CDP. Not one vendor website equals a CRM domain as delivered, so normalisation comes first and matching second. Both happen in plain SQL, lower, trim, strip and split, with no regex and no fuzzy scoring anywhere.

The four rules

  1. Only a key pointing at exactly one account may decide.
  2. The highest-precedence deciding key wins: LinkedIn slug, then website domain, then LinkedIn-reported domain.
  3. Every other key that matched must agree, or it is a conflict and nothing is assigned.
  4. Names never decide. A name pointing elsewhere costs 0.20 and holds the match for a steward.

LinkedIn leads because a subsidiary gets its own page while it often shares a parent's domain. That ordering is what separates Hooli from Hooli XYZ.

Memo

The six questions from the brief. Full text in MEMO.md.

Q1 Surface areas: deterministic, heuristic, or impossible?
SurfaceAttributionColumns it rests on
UIDeterministicraw_segment_pages.user_id, path
SDKDeterministic for 18 of 31 objects, heuristic for 1 more, and impossible to separate from the CLIrepo_info; metadata.ci_run_id / ci / source; raw_trace_volume (org × day)
CLIImpossible for usage; heuristic for intentraw_cli_wizard (a browser event); raw_api_keys.name containing "cli"

Nothing in the eighteen files names a client, user-agent or key id, so the CLI, which wraps the SDK, cannot be separated from it. The twelve objects carrying neither field are recorded unknown, not SDK.

Q2 How would you measure match quality, and precision or recall?

Five things, all recomputed by rpt_resolution_quality every run: precision on a monthly steward-labelled sample split by match_method, with a 98% bar for anything a rep sees; coverage, currently 8 of 11 companies carrying an account though only 7 deliver signals; corroboration, 4 of 8 matches resting on two or more independent keys; conflict and ambiguity rates as drift alarms; and how often reps click "wrong account".

Precision, every time. A bad merge sends a rep another company's news and costs the tool its credibility, and one of those is enough for a team to stop trusting the feed. A miss just sits in a queue where someone can still find it. That single stance produced all four precedence rules.

Q3 "What % of the account is using the CLI?"

Not truthfully, no. For Globex I would give three numbers: CLI usage is not measurable; setup intent covers 1 of 3 known product members, with a second not counted because he belongs to two accounts; and code-based usage by a person, which cannot be split between SDK and CLI, is 1 of 3, plus a CI job running under a member's key.

I would not give a CLI percentage, would not infer CLI from repo_info or key names, and would drop "% of the account" altogether, because the denominator is product members rather than headcount. cli_and_mcp_never_claimed_as_usage keeps that honest in code, and account_360.adoption_in_plain_english carries those exact sentences to the rep so the caveat travels with the number.

Q4 One instrumentation change, one data addition

Product. Captured at the API gateway from the client user-agent or MCP server id: stamp client_surface, client version and api_key_id on every write. Stored denormalised on the object tables and in a new api_request_log. CLI and MCP become deterministic, SDK separates from CLI, and service keys identify themselves instead of hiding behind whoever owns them.

CRM. Captured from an enrichment vendor at account creation and on refresh. Stored as required website_domain and linkedin_company_url on Account, plus a populated ParentId. Missing identifiers cause most non-matches, and the hierarchy lets Hooli and Hooli XYZ roll up without anything being merged.

Q5 What sales motion does this serve, and how would you know it works?

Expansion. Usage surges on non-Enterprise plans, CLI and MCP intent as an enablement opening, and funding, launch or hiring news. net_new_prospects feeds a product-led motion into sales alongside it.

Two leading indicators tell me whether it is working: do reps open the 360, and what share of signals get acted on rather than dismissed. Two lagging ones settle it: signal-sourced pipeline and expansion ARR, measured against a random holdout of accounts. A signal type with no lift, or more than roughly 70% dismissed, gets cut.

Q6 How would you find which signals actually drive value?
  1. Fix the outcome first: an opportunity, upgrade or expansion within 30 to 90 days.
  2. Snapshot fct_account_signals daily. It is already one shape, so it doubles as the feature store and joins straight to opportunities.
  3. Per signal type, measure lift over baseline controlled for plan and size, lead time, and precision at top-N. Then learn weights to replace the v0 rules while keeping the reasons visible, because a rep will not act on a score they cannot interrogate.
  4. Use holdouts so signals do not take credit for deals that would have closed anyway, treat rep dispositions as labels, and retire whatever shows no lift.

Limitations, and what I'd do next

What this does not do, stated plainly, because a platform whose whole argument is honesty about evidence should be honest about itself.

Known limitations

Snowflake is unverified. The project compiles for Snowflake and every compiled file passes a dialect check, but I had no live account to run it against. Three macros carry the genuine dialect differences.

The growth numbers sit on small bases. Traces cover eight days and are perfectly linear. Umbrella's +327% is arithmetically correct and practically meaningless.

Precision is asserted, not measured. There is no labelled match set, so I can report corroboration and conflict rates but not true precision.

No history. Everything is a current-state view, so nothing can yet answer what a rep saw and when.

Where I'd invest next, in order

1. Surface telemetry. client_surface and api_key_id on every write turns CLI and MCP from unmeasurable into deterministic, which is why it is first.

2. A labelled match set. 100 to 200 steward-verified pairs, plus a small review UI for the two queues that writes confirmed matches back.

3. History. Snapshot the 360 and the signal feed daily. It is the prerequisite for Q6 being answerable at all.

4. Account hierarchy. Parent and child, so Hooli and Hooli XYZ roll up without anything being merged.

5. Delivery. Reverse-ETL into Salesforce tasks or Slack, with a wrong-account button feeding straight back into the scorecard.

Production, if this stopped being a take-home. Only the raw layer changes: swap the seeds for landed tables, Fivetran for Salesforce, CDC or Snowpipe for usage, the vendor API for external, then repoint the 18 staging models from ref() to source(). Cleansed and unified do not change at all, which is the payoff of never letting business logic read a seed directly. The sources file is written and shipped at docs/sources.production.yml, with freshness thresholds per feed, so the claim is checkable rather than asserted.
